# Genesis Register: verified sources for the ideas that came before Bitcoin

Compiled 2026-10-31 for an educational exhibit. Accuracy comes before coverage.

**How this was checked.** Every page was read with a web-fetch tool, which pulls a passage out of the page on request. Quoted passages are as that tool returned them from the named page, and each is 15 words or fewer. Where a page could not be reached, the entry says so and gives no quote. Some links (bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf, metzdowd.com) could be reached only after a web search surfaced them. A few could not be reached at all (weidai.com, IEEE Xplore, ACM DL, dblp, web.archive.org, the cdn.nakamotoinstitute.org PDFs). Those gaps are noted where they matter.

**Connection types:**
- **whitepaper citation**: the work is in the reference list of the Bitcoin whitepaper.
- **documented influence**: a primary source documents the link.
- **context**: relevant history with no documented link.

## Entry 1: The Bitcoin whitepaper and its launch

**Event.** Satoshi Nakamoto, "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System." The author line reads "satoshin@gmx.com".

**Verified dates.**
- **Domain registered: 18 Aug 2008.**
  - The Bitcoin Wiki "Bitcoin.org" page cites a DomainTools WHOIS record retrieved 16 Jul 2015.
  - urlert.com's WHOIS-derived page shows "Registered: Aug 18, 2008".
  - Neither of these is a registry record that I could read directly. The bitcoin.org "Who owns" claim of co-registration by "Sirius" (Martti Malmi) is contradicted by Malmi, who says he joined in 2009.
- **Private draft: Aug 2008.** On 22 Aug 2008 Satoshi sent Wei Dai a draft titled "Electronic Cash Without a Trusted Third Party."
- **Public announcement: 31 Oct 2008, 14:10:00 EDT (18:10 UTC).** Posted to the cryptography list at metzdowd.com. The bitcoin.org PDF that the announcement links to carries no date of its own.
- **Genesis block: 2009-01-03 18:15:05 UTC.** This is the header timestamp (Unix 1231006505), which I converted myself. Blockchain.com shows "1/03/2009, 18:15:05 UTC".
  - Coinbase text: "The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks".
  - The header timestamp is not proof of when the block was actually mined. The Bitcoin Wiki notes that block 1 is timestamped six days later.
- **v0.1 release announcement: 8 Jan 2009, 19:27:40 UTC.** Nakamoto Institute archive. The Bitcoin Wiki "2009" page says 9 Jan.
  - The announcement's download link is http://downloads.sourceforge.net/bitcoin/bitcoin-0.1.0.rar
  - The Bitcoin Wiki says the SourceForge project was registered on 9 Nov 2008. I did not verify this.
- **First transaction to another person: block 170, 2009-01-12 03:30:25 UTC.**
  - The block is 00000000d1145790a8694403d4063f323d499e655c83426834d4ce2f8dd4a2ee and holds 2 transactions, per Blockchain.com.
  - Hal Finney later wrote (19 Mar 2013): "I was the recipient of the first bitcoin transaction."
  - The 10 BTC amount and the txid f4184fc5… come from secondary sources. I could not open the explorer's transaction page.

**Reference list [1]–[8], as printed in bitcoin.pdf.** Each entry was read on the page in two parts.

1. W. Dai, "b-money," http://www.weidai.com/bmoney.txt, 1998.
2. H. Massias, X.S. Avila, and J.-J. Quisquater, "Design of a secure timestamping service with minimal trust requirements," In 20th Symposium on Information Theory in the Benelux, May 1999.
3. S. Haber, W.S. Stornetta, "How to time-stamp a digital document," In Journal of Cryptology, vol 3, no 2, pages 99-111, 1991.
4. D. Bayer, S. Haber, W.S. Stornetta, "Improving the efficiency and reliability of digital time-stamping," In Sequences II: Methods in Communication, Security and Computer Science, pages 329-334, 1993.
5. S. Haber, W.S. Stornetta, "Secure names for bit-strings," In Proceedings of the 4th ACM Conference on Computer and Communications Security, pages 28-35, April 1997.
6. A. Back, "Hashcash - a denial of service counter-measure," http://www.hashcash.org/papers/hashcash.pdf, 2002.
7. R.C. Merkle, "Protocols for public key cryptosystems," In Proc. 1980 Symposium on Security and Privacy, IEEE Computer Society, pages 122-133, April 1980.
8. W. Feller, "An introduction to probability theory and its applications," 1957.

**Where each reference is used in the body:**
- [1] in §2: "transactions must be publicly announced [1]"
- [2]–[5] in §3: "such as in a newspaper or Usenet post [2-5]"
- [7], [2] and [5] in §7: "transactions are hashed in a Merkle Tree [7][2][5]"
- [6] in §4: "similar to Adam Back's Hashcash [6]"
- [8] in §11, the Gambler's Ruin calculation

**Contribution.** A peer-to-peer timestamp server that uses a proof-of-work chain. It removes the need for a trusted third party to prevent double-spending.

**Problem left unsolved.** Not applicable. This is the endpoint of the register.

**Primary sources.**
- https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf. Quote: "A purely peer-to-peer version of electronic cash would allow online payments" (truncated)
- https://www.metzdowd.com/pipermail/cryptography/2008-October/014810.html. Quote: "I've been working on a new electronic cash system that's fully peer-to-peer"
- https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/emails/cryptography/16/. Quote: "Announcing the first release of Bitcoin, a new electronic cash system" (truncated)
- https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/0 for the genesis block. Quote: "The Times 03/Jan/2009 Chancellor on brink of second bailout for banks"
- https://www.blockchain.com/explorer/blocks/btc/170
- https://nakamotoinstitute.org/bitcoin-and-me/
- https://en.bitcoin.it/wiki/Bitcoin.org for the domain date

**Confidence.**
- High: the paper, the 31 Oct announcement, the v0.1 post, and the genesis and block-170 timestamps.
- Medium: the domain date. It comes from WHOIS mirrors; I did not read a registry record.

**Open questions.**
- Was the Jan 2009 release on 8 Jan or 9 Jan? It depends on time zone and on whether you count the email or the SourceForge upload.
- The registrar and registrant of bitcoin.org were not verified.

## Entry 2: Wei Dai, "b-money" — whitepaper citation [1]

**Verified date.** Circulated in Nov 1998: the Nakamoto Institute dates the text to November 1998. Dai's own statement, in his reply to Satoshi published by gwern: "b-money was announced on the cypherpunks mailing list in 1998." I could not reach the original weidai.com/bmoney.txt or a 1998 cypherpunks archive message. A 25 Sep 1999 cypherpunks message from Adam Back ("eskimo.com archive of b-money is broke") shows that the text was circulating by 1999.

**Contribution.** Proposed money created by solving computational problems. In the first protocol every participant keeps a ledger and transfers are broadcast to everyone. In the second, a subset of servers keeps the accounts and must periodically publish their databases.

**Problem left unsolved.** It gave no practical way for anonymous participants to agree on a single ledger order. Appendix A offers an auction-based alternative for creating money. Bitcoin's proof-of-work chain provides the agreed ordering.

**Primary source.** https://nakamotoinstitute.org/b-money. Quotes:
- "I am fascinated by Tim May's crypto-anarchy."
- "Anyone can create money by broadcasting the solution to a previously unsolved computational problem."

The second quote is 15 words.

**Connection.** Whitepaper citation [1], plus documented influence (Entry 21).

**Confidence.** High that the work is cited and dates from 1998. Medium on the exact month.

**Open questions.**
- The exact date of the 1998 cypherpunks post.
- Satoshi's draft citation said "(2006?)" (see Entry 21). Dai supplied the 1998 date.

## Entry 3: Massias, Serret-Avila & Quisquater (1999) — whitepaper citation [2]

**Verified date.** Presented 27–28 May 1999 at the 20th Symposium on Information Theory in the Benelux, Haasrode, Belgium. The CORDIS event page gives those dates and the location; its date field shows only 27 May. The whitepaper says "May 1999".

**Contribution.** Design of a timestamping system for the Belgian TIMESEC project. It aims to minimise how much users must trust the timestamping authority by linking timestamps together and publishing them. That last point is from the abstract only; I could not read the full text.

**Problem left unsolved.** A timestamping service is still needed, so some operator remains. Bitcoin distributes the timestamp server.

**Primary sources.**
- https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/secure-timestamping-service lists May 1999 and gives no abstract.
- The PDF at cdn.nakamotoinstitute.org could not be fetched, so there is no quote from the paper.
- The Bitcoin Wiki essay page gives the abstract phrase "a timestamping system for the Belgian project TIMESEC".

**Connection.** Whitepaper citation [2].

**Confidence.**
- High on venue and year.
- Medium on content, because only the abstract was read.

**Open questions.** Get the full text from UCLouvain to confirm the linking scheme.

## Entry 4: Haber & Stornetta, "How to time-stamp a digital document" — whitepaper citation [3]

**Three separate dates. Do not merge them.**

| Date | What it represents |
|---|---|
| 11–15 Aug 1990 | Presented at **CRYPTO '90**, UC Santa Barbara. The dates are from the CiNii catalogue of the proceedings. |
| 1991 | Proceedings published as **LNCS 537** (eds. Menezes & Vanstone), pp. 437–455, Springer, ©1991, DOI 10.1007/3-540-38424-3_32. Springer's "Published: 18 May 2001" is only the date it was put online. |
| 1991 | Published in **Journal of Cryptology 3(2):99–111**, DOI 10.1007/BF00196791. The IACR volume index lists Vol. 3 No. 2 as 1991. The journal PDF shows "Date received: August 19, 1990. Date revised: October 26, 1990." |

The whitepaper cites the journal version.

**Contribution.** Timestamps each document by hashing it into a chain with the previous one, so that backdating would mean rewriting every later link. It also proposes a variant that uses distributed witnesses.

**Problem left unsolved.** The linking still relies on a service, or on widely witnessed publication, to fix the chain's head. Bayer, Haber & Stornetta (Entry 5) took up the cost of producing and storing timestamps one document at a time.

**Primary sources.**
- https://www.doi.org/10.1007/BF00196791 (abstract). Quote: "The prospect of a world in which all text, audio, picture, and video documents are"
- https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/3-540-38424-3_32
- https://ci.nii.ac.jp/ncid/BA13447188?l=en for the conference dates
- https://www.gwern.net/doc/bitcoin/1991-haber.pdf for the received date

**Connection.** Whitepaper citation [3].

**Confidence.** High.

**Open questions.** The month the 3(2) issue was printed is not stated on the pages I read.

## Entry 5: Bayer, Haber & Stornetta, "Improving the efficiency and reliability of digital time-stamping" — whitepaper citation [4]

**Verified date.**
- Presented at the workshop **Sequences '91**, Positano, Italy, **17–21 Jun 1991** (TAMU library catalogue note).
- The paper on Bayer's own site is dated **March 1992**.
- The book *Sequences II: Methods in Communication, Security, and Computer Science*, eds. Capocelli, De Santis & Vaccaro, was **published in 1993** by Springer-Verlag, New York. DOI chapter 10.1007/978-1-4613-9323-8_24.

**Contribution.** Gathers many documents into a binary hash tree in each round, so only one value per round has to be linked and published. This cuts storage and cost. It explicitly credits Merkle's 1980 hash trees ("[Merk 80]").

**Problem left unsolved.** A timestamping operator and periodic publication are still required.

**Primary sources.**
- https://www.math.columbia.edu/~bayer/papers/Timestamp_BHS93.pdf. Quote: "To establish that a document was created after a given moment in time, it is"
- https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-1-4613-9323-8_24
- https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00001114992/Description

**Connection.** Whitepaper citation [4]. This paper is also the documented route by which Merkle's 1980 work entered this line of research.

**Confidence.** High.

**Open questions.** None significant.

## Entry 6: Haber & Stornetta, "Secure names for bit-strings" — whitepaper citation [5]

**Verified date.** 4th ACM Conference on Computer and Communications Security (CCS '97), **Zurich, 1–4 Apr 1997**. Technical sessions ran 2–4 Apr.

**Connection.** Whitepaper citation [5].

**Confidence.** High on venue and date. Low on content detail.

**Open questions.** Confirm the DOI and the abstract on the ACM DL.

## Entry 7: Adam Back, Hashcash — whitepaper citation [6]

**Two separate dates.**
- **28 Mar 1997, 16:52:26 GMT.** "[ANNOUNCE] hash cash postage implementation," sent to cypherpunks@toad.com from aba@dcs.ex.ac.uk. It was read in the MIT archive copy of the cryptography list (msg 453). Back's reply dated 29 Mar 1997 is msg 439. The cypherpunks archive at venona (msg00774) could not be fetched.
- **1 Aug 2002.** The paper "Hashcash – A Denial of Service Counter-Measure"; the date is printed on the paper.
  - The paper's own reference [1] reads "Adam Back. Hashcash, May 1997." That date conflicts with the 28 Mar 1997 email (see the cautions section).
  - The paper also says "the author was not aware of the prior work by Dwork and Naor".

**Contribution.** A partial hash collision serves as a stamp that is costly to make, cheap to check, and has adjustable difficulty. It was meant as postage against spam.

**Problem left unsolved.** Hashcash stamps are spent once and cannot be transferred, so they are not money. RPOW (Entry 16) and Bitcoin reused the proof-of-work idea for transferable value and for ordering a ledger.

**Primary sources.**
- https://datwww.mit.edu/bloom-picayune/crypto/453. Quote: "can be made arbitrarily expensive to compute"
- https://cs.miami.edu/home/burt/learning/csc595.192/docs/hashcash.pdf (a mirror of hashcash.org/papers/hashcash.pdf, which redirected in a loop). Quote: "Hashcash was originally proposed as a mechanism to throttle systematic abuse of un-metered internet resources" — 15 words.

**Connection.** Whitepaper citation [6], plus documented influence:
- The 31 Oct 2008 announcement says "New coins are made from Hashcash style proof-of-work."
- On 15 Nov 2008 Satoshi wrote "It's a Hashcash style SHA-256 proof-of-work".
- Satoshi emailed Back on 20 Aug 2008 (Entry 21).

**Confidence.** High.

**Open questions.** Why does the 2002 paper say "May 1997"? It may refer to the cypherspace.org page date.

## Entry 8: Merkle, "Protocols for public key cryptosystems" (1980) — whitepaper citation [7]

**Verified date.** Presented at the 1980 IEEE Symposium on Security and Privacy, Oakland, CA, **14–16 Apr 1980**. Proceedings pp. 122–133. DOI 10.1109/SP.1980.10006. The date and venue are from the ISC bibliographic mirror of the IEEE record.

**What this paper is.** It is the tree-authentication (Merkle tree) line of work, not Merkle's puzzles.
- Its abstract compares "protocols for public key distribution and for digital signatures".
- Bayer, Haber & Stornetta cite "[Merk 80]" for hash trees.
- The whitepaper cites [7] only in §7: "transactions are hashed in a Merkle Tree [7][2][5]".
- Tree authentication is described in detail in Merkle's June 1979 Stanford thesis (Ch. V §4, "Tree Authentication"). It was patented as **US 4,309,569**, "Method of providing digital signatures": **filed 5 Sep 1979, granted 5 Jan 1982**, assigned to Stanford, expired 1999.

**Contribution.** A hash tree lets one root value authenticate many leaves using only a short path of hashes.

**Problem left unsolved.** Not a payments problem as such. Bitcoin uses the tree so that a node can discard spent transactions while the block hash stays valid (§7), and for simplified payment verification (§8).

**Primary sources.**
- https://search.isc.ac/inventory/49/2540503.htm (IEEE record mirror). Quote: "New Cryptographic protocols which take full advantage of the unique properties of public key cryptosystems" (15 words)
- https://www.ralphmerkle.com/papers/Thesis1979.pdf. Quote: "The method is called tree authentication because the computation of"
- https://patents.google.com/patent/US4309569 and https://ralphmerkle.com/merkleDir/patents.html. Quote: "filed September 5, 1979; issued January 5, 1982. Sole inventor."
- I could not read the full text of the 1980 paper because IEEE Xplore was blocked.

**Connection.** Whitepaper citation [7].

**Confidence.**
- High on the date and venue.
- Medium-high that the 1980 paper contains the tree method. This rests on the thesis, the patent and BHS93's citation of it, not on reading the 1980 paper itself.

**Open questions.** Read the 1980 paper's tree-authentication section directly.

### Not to be confused with Merkle's puzzles (context only)

- **Fall 1974:** CS244 project proposal at UC Berkeley, rejected by the instructor. Merkle's 1974 page says he rediscovered the original seven-page proposal on 8 Sep 2005.
- **Aug 1975:** submitted to CACM. **Dec 1975:** revision dated 7 Dec 1975.
- **Apr 1978:** published as "Secure communications over insecure channels," **CACM 21(4):294–299** (received Aug 1975, revised Sep 1977).

**Sources.**
- https://ralphmerkle.com/1974/PuzzlesAsPublished.pdf. Quote: "According to traditional conceptions of cryptographic security, it is necessary to transmit a key, by"
- https://www.ralphmerkle.com/1974/. Quote: "almost three years of delay"

**Connection.** Context. See Entry 24(c).

## Entry 9: Feller (1957) — whitepaper citation [8]

## Entry 10: Diffie & Hellman, "New directions in cryptography"

**Verified date.** Journal issue: **IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, IT-22(6):644–654, November 1976**. Manuscript received 3 Jun 1976.

**Contribution.** Introduced public-key cryptography and key exchange in the open literature, and proposed digital signatures.

**Problem left unsolved.** Gave no practical signature scheme. RSA (Entry 11) supplied one. The signatures that Bitcoin uses descend from this line of work.

**Primary source.** https://ee.stanford.edu/~hellman/publications/24.pdf (Hellman's own site). Quote: "We stand today on the brink of a revolution in cryptography."

**Connection.** Context.

**Confidence.** High.

**Open questions.** None.

## Entry 12: Chaum, "Blind signatures for untraceable payments" and DigiCash

**Verified dates.**
- **23–25 Aug 1982:** presented at the CRYPTO '82 workshop, UC Santa Barbara.
- **1983:** proceedings published as *Advances in Cryptology: Proceedings of Crypto 82*, Plenum Press, New York (TAMU catalogue).
- Chaum's own publications page cites it as "Plenum Press, pp. 199–203, 1982". That is the conference year.

**DigiCash.**
- Wired 2.12 (Dec 1994): "So, in 1990, he launched DigiCash b.v., a subsidiary of the US company DigiCash Inc."
- Wikipedia says "founded by David Chaum in 1989" and cites no source for that year.
- Bitcoin Magazine says "By 1990". I am treating 1990 as the better-attested year, at medium confidence.
- Wikipedia gives the bankruptcy as 1998. I did not verify that against a primary source.

**Contribution.** Blind signatures let a bank sign coins it cannot later link to the payer, which gives untraceable digital cash.

**Problem left unsolved.** A central mint is still needed, and that mint can fail or be shut down. Bitcoin removes the mint. Satoshi alluded to this in Feb 2009 (Entry 22).

**Primary sources.**
- https://chaum.com/publications/ (the author's own site). Quote: "Blind Signatures for Untraceable Payments," D. Chaum, Advances in Cryptology – Proceedings of CRYPTO '82
- https://libcat.library.tamu.edu/Record/in00000372819/Description
- https://web.stanford.edu/~joelaw/bennpaper/publicdomain/wiredemoney.html (a copy of the Wired article)
- The paper PDF on chaum.com returned binary data, so I have no quote from the paper.

**Connection.** Context. Satoshi's "old Chaumian central mint stuff" (15 Feb 2009) refers to Chaum but describes it as something Bitcoin is not, so it is context rather than influence.

**Confidence.** High for the paper. Medium for DigiCash's founding year.

**Open questions.** Find a primary source for DigiCash's founding: Dutch Chamber of Commerce records or a contemporary press release.

## Entry 13: Dwork & Naor, "Pricing via processing or combatting junk mail"

## Entry 16: Hal Finney, RPOW (Reusable Proofs of Work)

**Verified date.** Announced **15 Aug 2004, 10:43:09 PDT** on the cryptography list (metzdowd). R. A. Hettinga forwarded it the same day (MIT archive msg 15913).

**Contribution.** A server running on tamper-resistant IBM 4758 hardware swaps one Hashcash token for a new one. That makes proof-of-work transferable, so it can be passed on like a coin.

**Problem left unsolved.** Users still had to trust a central server, even with remote attestation. Bitcoin replaced the server with a distributed chain.

**Primary source.** https://www.metzdowd.com/pipermail/cryptography/2004-August/007362.html. Quote: "I'd like to invite members of this list to try out my new hashcash-based server" — 15 words.

**Connection.** Context.
- I found no Satoshi text that cites RPOW.
- Finney himself (19 Mar 2013) recalled trying to "create my own proof of work based currency, called RPOW."

**Confidence.** High on the date.

**Open questions.** None.

## Entry 17: Nick Szabo, bit gold — the chronology

**What sources say, in order.**

1. **1998 (claimed).** Szabo, "Bitcoin, what took ye so long?" (28 May 2011): "I came up with them in 1998" and "on the same private mailing list where Dai was coming up with b-money." Wikipedia's 1998 date cites IEEE Spectrum (May 2012). I found **no 1998 primary text** on bit gold.
2. **Dec 2005 (first posting).** The blog post's permalink is /2005/12/bit-gold.html.
   - The Nakamoto Institute dates the essay **29 Dec 2005**.
   - The first comments on the post are dated 29–31 Dec 2005, which places the original posting in late December 2005. The fetch tool gave both 29 and 30 Dec for Szabo's own "Thanks!" reply, probably because of time zones.
   - The Bitcoin Wiki talk page cites a Wayback Machine snapshot from 29 Mar 2006 at that URL. I could not fetch web.archive.org.
3. **Displayed date today: Saturday, 27 Dec 2008, 4:16 PM.**
   - Read directly on https://unenumerated.blogspot.com/2005/12/bit-gold.html and on the blog's /2008/12 archive.
   - The companion post "Bit gold markets" has a /2008/04/ permalink with comments from 9 Apr 2008, but it also shows the date 27 Dec 2008, 4:17 PM.
   - So both posts were re-dated, or re-published, to 27 Dec 2008. Their permalinks keep the original months.
   - A Medium response (could not be fetched; known only from its search-result title) says the post was "actually written in 2005" and later "altered" to show December 2008. Gwern's page notes Szabo "blogging and fielding questions about bit gold on … December 27, 2008."
   - Popular accounts that date the post "27 Dec 2005" appear to merge the 2005 permalink with the 2008 display date. No source I read shows that exact date.

**Contribution.** Proposed proof-of-work strings ("bit gold miners" computing puzzle solutions), chained and timestamped: a scarce digital commodity that does not depend on a trusted issuer. The "Bit gold markets" post opens: "The basic idea of bit gold is for "bit gold miners" to set their computers…"

**Problem left unsolved.** Bit gold units are not interchangeable, because each unit's value depends on its mining cost. Ownership was to be recorded in a separate property-title registry rather than ordered by the proof-of-work itself. Bitcoin gives each coin the same value and uses the proof-of-work chain itself for consensus.

The detailed mechanism comes from general knowledge of the essay. In this session I read only its opening lines.

**Primary sources.**
- https://unenumerated.blogspot.com/2005/12/bit-gold.html. Quote: "A long time ago I hit upon the idea of bit gold."
- https://nakamotoinstitute.org/bit-gold/ (gives 29 Dec 2005)
- https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bitcoin-what-took-ye-so-long/

**Connection.** Documented influence, on Satoshi's own words of 20 Jul 2010 (Entry 23). This is a retrospective description: the whitepaper does not cite bit gold, and Satoshi's 2008 emails to Dai do not mention it. Hal Finney's list message of 7 Nov 2008 notes that Szabo "wrote many years ago about what he called 'bit gold'."

**Confidence.**
- High that the post was first published in late Dec 2005 and now displays 27 Dec 2008.
- Low on the 1998 conception date, which rests on Szabo's own later statement.

The register takes no position on why the post was re-dated.

**Open questions.**
- Retrieve Wayback snapshots from 2006–2008 to see which date the post showed before Dec 2008.
- Find the 1998 private-list material, if any survives.

## Discrepancies and cautions

1. **Haber & Stornetta, "1991".**
   - The work was presented at CRYPTO '90 in **Aug 1990**.
   - Both the LNCS 537 proceedings and the J. Cryptology 3(2) version are **1991**.
   - The whitepaper cites the journal version.
   - Springer's "Published 18 May 2001" is only the date it went online.
2. **Bayer, Haber & Stornetta, "1993".**
   - The workshop (Sequences '91, Positano) was **June 1991**.
   - The paper on the author's site is dated March 1992.
   - The book came out in **1993**.
3. **Hashcash, "1997" vs "2002".**
   - The idea was announced on **28 Mar 1997**.
   - The cited paper is dated **1 Aug 2002**.
   - The 2002 paper itself dates the original proposal to "May 1997". That conflicts with the archived 28 Mar 1997 email.
4. **Merkle, three different things:**
   - Puzzles: proposed in 1974, published in CACM in April 1978.
   - Tree authentication: thesis June 1979, patent filed 5 Sep 1979 and granted 5 Jan 1982, IEEE S&P paper April 1980.
   - The whitepaper cites only the April 1980 paper.
   - The Nakamoto Institute's library page for "Protocols for Public Key Cryptosystems" shows **"April 1978"**. That is wrong for this paper: its own DOI link is SP.1980.10006. The date appears to have been mixed up with the CACM puzzles paper.
5. **Chaum, 1982 vs 1983.** The conference was August 1982; the Plenum proceedings came out in 1983. Chaum's own site gives 1982.
6. **DigiCash, 1989 vs 1990.** Wired (Dec 1994) says DigiCash b.v. was launched in 1990. Wikipedia's 1989 has no source.
7. **Bit gold, "1998" / "2005" / "2008".**
   - 1998 is Szabo's own later claim, with no 1998 text found.
   - The post was first published in **Dec 2005** (the Nakamoto Institute says 29 Dec 2005).
   - The blog currently **displays 27 Dec 2008**.
   - "27 Dec 2005" as a single date is not supported by any source I read.
8. **b-money, "1998" vs "2006?".** Satoshi's Aug 2008 draft guessed 2006. Dai corrected it to 1998, and the final paper says 1998.
9. **Whitepaper date.**
   - The whitepaper PDF is undated.
   - It was announced on 31 Oct 2008 at 18:10 UTC.
   - The Nakamoto Institute "Quotable Satoshi" page dates the "Hashcash style proof-of-work" quote to 1 Nov 2008. The thread archive and metzdowd put that message at 31 Oct 2008, so the 1 Nov date appears to be an archive artefact.
10. **v0.1 release, 8 vs 9 Jan 2009.** The mailing-list post was 8 Jan 2009 at 19:27:40 UTC. The Bitcoin Wiki says 9 Jan.
11. **Genesis timestamp.** 2009-01-03 18:15:05 UTC is the timestamp in the block header. It is not independent proof of when the block was mined; block 1 is timestamped 9 Jan.
12. **Massias et al.** The whitepaper says "May 1999". The symposium was held 27–28 May 1999 in Haasrode. The author's name is Serret-Avila, which the whitepaper prints as "X.S. Avila".
13. **"Secure names for bit-strings"** appeared in the preliminary CCS '97 program under the title "Secure Digital Names".
14. **Surety "since 1995"** comes from secondary sources only. Surety's own pages say "founded in 1994" and give no start year for the NYT ads.
15. **Cypherpunks "1992".** The group formed in September 1992 (per the Cyphernomicon). The exact date of the first list message was not verified.
16. **bitcoin.org, 18 Aug 2008.** This date comes from WHOIS mirrors and the Bitcoin Wiki (citing DomainTools). It was not read from the registry.
