# Era 5: Peers and Reusable Work (2000–2007)

## Summary for a 12-year-old (3–5 sentences)
Computers were learning to find one another and share the work. Sharing a file, however, is different from agreeing who owns money. Hal Finney tried reusable proof-of-work tokens; Nick Szabo described costly digital objects with recorded ownership. These experiments exposed problems rather than making Bitcoin inevitable. [E5-CHORD, E5-BT, E5-RPOW, E5-BG]

## The deeper story (2–4 paragraphs)
Make this room a workshop with several competing benches, not a conveyor belt of inevitable inventions. Chord locates information across changing peers; BitTorrent distributes file pieces. Neither establishes a monetary ledger. Their relationship here is context, not a demonstrated Bitcoin borrowing. [E5-CHORD, E5-BT]

The money benches compare different trust boundaries. Hashcash makes work costly to produce and cheap to check. RPOW makes tokens reusable through a server whose software configuration can be attested. Bit gold proposes timestamped work and a distributed ownership registry, while acknowledging unequal production costs. A later Satoshi statement credits bit gold, but it does not establish when particular design ideas were encountered. [E5-HC, E5-RPOW, E5-BG, E5-ATTR]

## Exhibits (Bitcoin line)

### Find the right peer
- **Date:** 27–31 August 2001, SIGCOMM conference dates printed on the Chord paper; not a claim that all DHTs began then.
- **Contribution:** Key-to-node lookup despite peers joining and leaving.
- **Problem left unsolved:** Lookup alone does not agree ownership or prevent double-spending.
- **Primary source / passage:** [E5-CHORD: Chord paper](https://pdos.csail.mit.edu/papers/chord:sigcomm01/chord_sigcomm.pdf), “given a key, it maps the key onto a node.”
- **Connection:** context; no proof of direct Bitcoin influence established here.
- **Confidence / open questions:** High on paper and conference; first-release chronology of competing DHT implementations is outside this exhibit.

### Share the pieces
- **Date:** 22 May 2003, date printed on Bram Cohen's technical paper, not BitTorrent's launch date.
- **Contribution:** Downloaders exchange file pieces and distribute upload costs.
- **Problem left unsolved:** File replication does not provide monetary consensus.
- **Primary source / passage:** [E5-BT: Incentives Build Robustness in BitTorrent](https://www.bittorrent.org/bittorrentecon.pdf), “they upload pieces of the file to each other.”
- **Connection:** context.
- **Confidence / open questions:** High for the paper; do not print a precise 2001 launch date until a dated release artifact is registered.

### The work-stamp manual
- **Date:** 1 August 2002, date printed on Adam Back's paper. Earlier Hashcash proposals belong in Era 4.
- **Contribution:** Collects and analyzes computational cost-functions and Hashcash applications.
- **Problem left unsolved:** A service still checks spent stamps; this is not Bitcoin's shared payment history.
- **Primary source / passage:** [E5-HC: Hashcash paper](https://cdn.nakamotoinstitute.org/docs/hashcash.pdf), “A cost-function should be efficiently verifiable, but parameterisably expensive to compute.”
- **Connection:** whitepaper citation, reference [6], verified in [E5-WP](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf), reference page and section 4.
- **Confidence / open questions:** High. The paper retrospectively says May 1997; do not silently replace a separately sourced March announcement date with that recollection.

### Reuse a token
- **Date:** 15 August 2004, 10:43:09 PDT, Finney's announcement email.
- **Contribution:** Exchanges spent proof-of-work tokens for fresh signed tokens while rejecting reuse of inputs.
- **Problem left unsolved:** Depends on an operating server and its attested trusted hardware; early beta resets could invalidate tokens.
- **Primary source / passage:** [E5-RPOW: original announcement, SNI reprint](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/rpow/), “But they are reusable, unlike hashcash.”
- **Connection:** context; not a whitepaper reference and no direct influence claim established.
- **Confidence / open questions:** High for announced design; deployment reliability is not established by this announcement.

### Costly bits, recorded owners
- **Date:** 29 December 2005, date SNI assigns to its Bit Gold text; exact first-publication dating remains qualified.
- **Contribution:** Combines work, timestamps and an ownership registry in a proposal for scarce digital objects.
- **Problem left unsolved:** Different hardware and production periods make units unequal; trust and registry design remain important.
- **Primary source / passage:** [E5-BG: Szabo's essay, SNI reprint](https://nakamotoinstitute.org/library/bit-gold/), “The last-created string of bit gold provides the challenge bits”.
- **Connection:** documented influence, qualified retrospective attribution. [E5-ATTR: Satoshi, 20 July 2010 forum post](https://satoshi.nakamotoinstitute.org/posts/bitcointalk/249/) calls Bitcoin an implementation of b-money and “Nick Szabo's Bitgold proposal”. This does not prove a contemporaneous sequence of borrowing.
- **Confidence / open questions:** High for proposal content and later attribution; medium for exact original publication date. The [author's current page](https://unenumerated.blogspot.com/2005/12/bit-gold.html) displays 27 December 2008 despite its 2005 URL; archival dating needs resolution before an unqualified date plaque.

## Period gear to play with
- **Portable hard-drive music player:** Announced 23 October 2001; available 10 November. Apple's original player had a **5 GB** hard drive. [E5-GEAR: manufacturer announcement](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2001/10/23Apple-Presents-iPod/). Mechanic: sort original synthesized sound packets into a capacity bar. Model a generic box with arrow buttons, no logos, copied wheel or recorded songs.
- **Attested server cabinet:** RPOW's 2004 deployment used an **IBM 4758**, described as **FIPS 140 level 4**. [E5-RPOW]. This dates its documented use, not the hardware's introduction. Mechanic: compare the code fingerprint before accepting a token. Draw a generic sealed cabinet and fictional indicator lights; never imply the museum performs genuine hardware attestation.

## Music direction
Creative direction, not a claim of historical influence: restrained downtempo electronics, soft drum machine, plucked synth and warm electric-piano timbres; 88–96 BPM. Original motifs: a two-note request answered across the room; an irregular work pulse becoming a reusable rhythm; a rising four-note figure that never quite resolves.
